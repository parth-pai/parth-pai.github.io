Hello everyone, this is a blog explaining my experience during the masters application for Fall 2026. I will be joining University of California San Diego for my Masters of Science in Data Science from Fall 2026. 

It was the end of third year, coming back to India from my exchange program and starting my internship at JLR. Although the internship was pretty good, the work was slightly saturated and didn't see enough growth in career or other aspects after discussing with many people. This influenced my decision of pursuing a masters degree abroad for a rich academic experience and possible career prospects. (Also my sister was in the US, which slightly influenced my decision). I decided to lock in and start MS application prep towards the end of my internship in early July of 2025. 

I mostly contacted a couple of seniors, grad mentors, grad booklet by ugac for advice and grad resources. I also followed the amazing blogs by [Kalpesh Krishna](https://martiansideofthemoon.github.io/) for GRE and ToEFL advice, along with casual reddit gyan. You can find all my masters resources [here](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1XiS6PFSLthbkImYFO8aCLMTZvj0eGNBd?usp=sharing)

# General Timeline 
- July End: Start shortlisting universities and countries and finalise on which ones need GRE / ToEFL / any other exams. The shortlisted universities could incur changes again and again.
- September 1st week: Get done with GRE atleast.
- September 2nd week: Get done with ToEFL / other exams and Start with CV
- September 3rd week: Start requesting professors / companies for LORs and start drafting rough SOP and LORs.
- October end: Clear picture of your LOR professors and SOP.
- November 2nd week: Start all the application links, fill them and submit soon. 

These timelines might shift if you are also applying for Placements / can get shifted due to midsems etc.

# Things needed for the application

Firstly your application majorly depends on your cpi (I know people with 8-8.5 cpi as well who got MS admits), SOP and LORs. Some universities have a requirement of undergraduate courses to be completed. You can show that in the form of courses done / similar courses done during your stay at IITB. It's generally hard to tell what was the reason for a university to accept since their rejection mails are quite standard.

### Details about crafting an SOP

- First paragraph - general / overall interest in the field, why do you want to pursue it, brief idea about what you would do in the future and how the corresponding Masters / PhD degree will help here.
- Second paragraph to penultimate paragraph - Take 2-3 projects (that includes LOR preferably) and explain them very well. don't go into too much of technicalities, explain it briefly and how it shaped your interest in the field. Be as specific as possible. Being specific gives an idea to the institute that you are very clear about your goals and interests and hence you are pursuing higher studies. Being specific is weighted more in a PhD application as compared to Masters application though.
- Final paragraph (different for diff univ) - explain which courses / labs / professors you are interested in working with and why, and hence this degree in this institute will be helpful. Make sure to mention the prof that you want to be your advisor in the case of PhD application. The profs will surely evaluate your application in that case.

Note that all the paragraphs except for the last one, can be common for all universities (except for First para saying "I'm interested in _____ degree in _____ instutute). 

Write your SOPs in Latex, it will look good. Mention your email address and website link as well in the top, again this will look good. Use a simple looking template for SOP with good enough margin space etc. I like to use a font similar to Times New Roman. Generally keep SOP as 1.5 to 2 pages. It doesn't need to be from end to end or filling entire page. 

I used [this](https://www.overleaf.com/read/znchzsjckyyp#8f750e) template for my SOP.



### Details about LORs
Generally universities ask for 3 LORs, with >= 2 Academic LORs (can be TA / Research work / grades - slightly weaker) and <= 1 industry LOR (better if you did research work there). Professors and industry people generally don't have enough time to draft an LOR. So you can go ahead, draft an LOR and send the professor. They will generally make some minor changes, sign it and submit it to the university. **MAKE SURE TO WAIVE THE RIGHTS TO VIEW THE LOR DOCUMENT**. Not waving the same will give bad impression for the professor and for the admitting institute. And ask the professors / industry to send it with a header / seal and with official email id.

You are free to exaggerate since you will write the draft (don't do it too much) and make sure to say that you were very helpful in the project because of this aspect and these technical skills. This phrasing will generally stand out. Can write them in Google Doc itself.



### Details about CV 
CV is not very important for Masters application but it is extremely important for a PhD application. The Professors will keep your CV and take your inteview in case of the latter. Don't make it like a normal IITB placement / internship resume. You are not expected to do GPT and write lines such that it covers end to end of a line and span only 1 line / 2 line. There are no such restrictions. The CV doesn't need to be only 2 page or something. Can have more than 2 pages. 
Start with your name, contact, location etc. Then keep sections like this: Education,  Research Interests, Publications and/or patents and/or conferences you attended (if any), Scholastic achievements (can be later if projects are better), Research Projects, Technical Skills, Teaching experience, Relevant courses, Extracurriculars & community service.  

It is recommended to use Latex for CV. I used [this](https://www.overleaf.com/read/yqryybvknnhj#0a6395) template for my CV (minus the photo).



### Preparing for exams 
[GRE](https://www.in.ets.org/gre/test-takers.html): Take the GRE General test. Math part was easy, just be accurate. For the english part, I took [Gregmat](https://www.gregmat.com/) subscription for about 2 months and did words / paragraphs and other questions from there. It was pretty helpful. End of the day, you need to memorise as many words as possible (about 600 words ideally). I used Barrons 333,  Flashcards by Magoosh and Gregmat's 800 word list. For questions, keep on practicing with the officials books and big book. The official GRE website has 2 sample mock tests. They are actual GRE level tests tbh. The first one is untimed and second one is timed. Take the first one few days before the test (like 2 weeks) and second test about 5 days before the test. The rest of the mock tests were from Gregmat. 
This exam needs atleast 1 - 1.5 month preparation if you don't read novels / write blogs multiple times. (I personally hated GRE :/). There is some chinese websites like [this](https://www.gregmat.com/) where you can take free mock tests from.

[ToEFL](https://www.ets.org/toefl.html): This was easy, has 4 sections - Reading, Writing, Listening and Speaking. Take some mock tests and you will be good to go (approx 4-5 days prep time is good enough). Took mocktests from [Testglider](https://www.testglider.com/toefl/en/mock-tests)



### Other important documents 
Latest Transcript, Passport which is pretty recent (else it might cause issues during visa / stay in the foreign country), 10th 12th markscard, Exchange transcript (if any).



### Application Decisions 
PhD interviews start to come by Jan start and can go until February Mid. One gets PhD decisions during that time only. Only after that one gets Masters decisions. I started getting my rejections from February itself and going upto April End. My UCSD MS DS accept was in Feb 3rd week, NYU MS DS accept was in March 1st week and UMich MS IOE accept was in April End. I chose UCSD MS DS out of the three options. 



### Specifically for US universities & for international students
There are a couple of things to do once you get an admit and choose a particular university.
1. Accept the university that you are interested in. For some of them, you gave to pay some deposit to accept it. 
2. Start finding for a Loan from and bank, generally HDFC Credilla, Leap Finance, IDFC bank etc works. Better to take loan in INR since repayment will be better (due to INR to USD depreciation)
3. Once you get your loan sanctioned, request for i20 from the university. The i20 will have approximate cost for 1 year in that institute. Getting i20 depends on the university. Generally it takes 2-3 weeks. I was extremely lucky and got it in one day lol.
4. Once you get i20, start filling DS160 form, submit it along with SEVIS number, start applying for appointments in US visa scheduling website [here](https://www.usvisascheduling.com/).
5. Parallely, pay the mandatory $350 SEVIS fee for all students in [this](https://www.fmjfee.com/i901fee/index.htm) and print this i901 form.
6. Give biometrics, during interview day, get ready with DS160 submission document, sevis fee paid receipt (1901 basically), visa appointment email printout, passport, loan documents, i20 and acceptance letter. Your visa decision will come up in couple of days and if approved you can pick it up there in the consulate or ask them to courier it to your address for extra fees of about 1000 INR.
7. Book flights and go to the US!
